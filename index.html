Six projects from the last three years. Each one shows the problem, what I did and what changed as a result.

012025 to present
CompleteSDK ongoingOpen source

Bringing lab instruments onto PyLabRobot

Role
Proposed the approach to the R&D Director, then built it
Tools
Python, PyLabRobot, serial protocol capture, legacy instrument runtime

PyLabRobot is a free, open source Python framework for lab automation. It lets scientists control robots from different makers with the same code. Most lab robots only run on their maker's own software, which locks labs in and makes methods hard to share.

I made an automated liquid dispenser fully compatible with PyLabRobot. The dispenser was a legacy serial device with only an old manual. I captured its live serial traffic and found the timing problems that were causing lost commands. It now runs from Python as a tested driver.

I am now leading a Python SDK for our liquid handling robots. I designed it to sit on top of the existing control software through a defined interface. That way, proven code already serving customers does not need to change.

Why it mattersLabs using open tools can add these instruments without buying into a closed system, and methods become easier to repeat and share.

02May 2026 to present
Certification in progressMachine safety

Safety testing and certification of a liquid handling Cartesian robot

Standards
EN ISO 12100, Machinery Directive, CE marking
Outputs
Test plan, test records, NCRs, component compliance tracker

I own the preparation of a liquid handling Cartesian robot for third party safety certification with Eurofins. I wrote the mechanical safety test plan of 32 tests and directed the testing.

During testing I found that any magnet could defeat the guard interlock. That was a critical safety risk. I restricted use of the machine and raised a non-conformance. I instructed the design engineer to redesign it with a SICK reed switch. I closed the NCR only after a verified re-test.

I also gather compliance certificates from manufacturers and suppliers for every critical component, and track them against the certification evidence.

Why it mattersThe robot is used by lab staff every day, and a guard that anyone can bypass puts them at risk.

Replacing a decades old MS-DOS system at a biopharma lab

Role
Requirements, protocols, testing, on-site commissioning, training

A biopharma lab in London was still running its gel punching process on an MS-DOS system. I worked with the customer to define their requirements and wrote the protocols for the new automated system.

I tested parallel and series vacuum set-ups to find which gave the better performance. I then commissioned the system at the customer's lab and trained their staff to use it.

ResultA faster Windows based system with run logging, on supported hardware the lab can maintain.

04Jun 2023 to Jan 2025
In daily useManufacturing

Condition monitoring programme for a manufacturing plant

Role
Programme lead and budget holder
Tools
AVEVA Insight, Schneider TM262 with TM3, Modbus, MQTT, Power BI

I led an anomaly based condition monitoring programme across five production areas of a working plant, and held its budget. I set the scope using an FMECA of 251 plant assets, ranked across five criticality levels.

I specified Schneider TM262 PLCs and sensor networks, brought plant data in over Modbus and MQTT, and built a data historian with Power BI reporting. I worked under permit-to-work in ATEX zones and wrote the risk assessments and method statements for installation.

The system gave early warning of a bearing problem on the thermal oil system that the existing SCADA did not show. I took the programme from pilot stage to day-to-day use by process, production and maintenance engineers. I also saved over £2,000 by learning AVEVA Insight through free resources instead of paid training.

05Lab automation
HardwareDiagnostics

Hardware design and fault finding on lab robots

Parts and methods
Allegro A9102, RC filter design, LUT stepper control, protocol and log analysis

I designed a cover sensor circuit around an Allegro A9102 linear Hall effect sensor. I removed parts of the original schematic that were distorting the analog signal and added an RC low-pass filter to cut ADC noise.

I worked out stepper motor motion profiles for the X, Y and Z axes from the controller's speed lookup table. When a robot showed intermittent faults, I decoded its runtime logs and traced the cause to a wireless link with an accessory module.

I also led an investigation into barcode read failures. I found the cause was label print quality, and had the thermal print settings changed in production.
